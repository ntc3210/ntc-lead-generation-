# The interview: questions that work in any industry

This is how Lead Scout learns a business. Ask **one question at a time**
(two at most if they're closely related). Offer example answers so the
user can just pick one. Look up public answers yourself and ask the user
to confirm them, rather than making them type out what's already on their
website.

Save answers to `my-company/profile.md` (template:
`templates/company-profile.md`). Label each fact **said by user**,
**found at <URL>**, or **my guess**.

The questions are grouped in stages. Stages 1 to 4 are enough to start
finding leads. Stages 5 to 8 sharpen the search, so ask them while you
work and don't hold everything up for them.

---

## Stage 1: Who you are (always first)

1. **Which company do you work for, and what's your role there?**
   *Why: everything else depends on it. Your role also tells me what you
   can approve yourself.*
   - Then look up their website. Read the home, product, pricing,
     customers and careers pages. Summarise what you found in three lines
     and ask "Did I get that right?"
2. **In one sentence, what do you sell?** (Offer the summary you just
   made as a draft for them to correct.)
3. **Is it a product, a service, software, or a mix?** Is it bought once,
   by subscription, or by usage?
4. **Roughly how big is the company?** Just you, under 50, 50–500, or
   bigger? *Why: it tells me how much outreach you can actually handle.*

## Stage 2: Who buys

5. **Who are your best three customers today, and why are they the
   best?** *Why: the fastest way to find more good customers is to look
   for companies like your best ones.* If they can't name them, ask for
   the industry and size, or look at the logos and case studies on their
   website.
6. **Which industries buy from you most?** Offer examples drawn from
   their website.
7. **What size are those companies?** (Startups, mid-size, enterprise,
   government?)
8. **Where in the world?** Are there countries or regions you can't sell
   to?
9. **Inside the customer, who uses it, who decides, and who signs?**
   - User: the person who works with it day to day.
   - Champion: the person who pushes for it internally.
   - Decider: the person who says yes.
   - Signer: the person who controls the budget.
   Give example job titles for their industry.
10. **Who is NOT a good customer?** These might be too small, the wrong
    industry, competitors, or people who always churn. *Why: knowing what
    to skip saves you more time than anything else.*

## Stage 3: The problem and the moment

11. **What problem does a customer have right before they buy from you?**
    Ask them to use the customer's own words if they can ("our
    transcripts are wrong", "we're missing calls after hours").
12. **What usually happens that makes a company start looking?** For
    example a new hire, new funding, a new law, a failed tool, growth, a
    complaint, a launch, or a busy season. *Why: these "moments" are the
    signals I search for.* See `guide/lead-signals.md`.
13. **What were they using before you?** (A competitor, doing it by hand,
    building it themselves, or nothing.)
14. **Who are your main competitors, and why do people pick you over
    them?**

## Stage 4: The deal and what you're allowed to do

15. **What is a typical customer worth?** First order, then per year.
    Ranges are fine. *Why: it decides how much effort each lead deserves.
    A $50 sale and a $50,000 contract need very different outreach.*
16. **How long does it take from first contact to a signed deal?**
17. **Which ways of reaching people are you allowed and willing to use?**
    Show the menu: company websites, cold email, phone calls (a person
    dials), LinkedIn by hand, Reddit and forums, news, job posts, events,
    partners, content. Ask whether their company has rules about outreach.
18. **What will you send from?** Their own work email, a separate
    outreach domain, or nothing yet? What postal address goes in the
    emails? (Commercial email legally needs one; see
    `guide/channels/cold-email.md`.)
19. **How many people can you realistically contact a week?** 10, 50, or
    200? *Why: I'll find leads at the pace you can actually follow up.*

## Stage 5: What you've tried

20. **What have you already tried to get customers, and what happened?**
21. **Where did your current customers come from?** Referrals, search,
    events, partners, outbound?
22. **Any message or pitch that has worked well?** Paste it if you have
    one.

## Stage 6: Where your buyers spend time

23. **Where do your buyers go to learn or ask for help?** Forums,
    subreddits, Slack or Discord groups, newsletters, podcasts,
    conferences, trade bodies. If they don't know, offer to research it
    and come back with a list (as in `examples/deepgram/profile.md`,
    section 6).
24. **Are there partners your buyers already use** that you work with
    or could? For example platforms, marketplaces, resellers or agencies.

## Stage 7: Proof you can show

25. **What results can you share publicly?** Numbers, case studies,
    reviews, logos. *Why: outreach with real proof gets replies;
    outreach without it gets ignored. I will never invent proof.*
26. **Is there anything you must never say or claim?** For example
    compliance lines, unannounced features, or customer names under
    NDA.

## Stage 8: Practical setup (only when needed)

27. **Do you want me to search on my own later?** That needs a Brave
    Search API key and costs about $5 per 1,000 searches, with $5 free a
    month (check https://brave.com/search/api/ for today's price). If yes,
    walk them through it step by step, with exact clicks.
28. **Do you want help sending emails after you approve them?** That
    needs an email-sending setup. Walk them through it step by step, and
    tell them the cost before they commit.

---

## When the user doesn't know an answer

- Offer to find out: "Want me to look at your website, case studies or
  job posts and suggest an answer?"
- Offer three example answers to pick from.
- Write down "unknown, revisit" and move on. Never stall the whole
  interview on one question.

## When the answers change

People correct themselves. Update `my-company/profile.md`. If the change
alters who you were targeting, tell the user which saved leads no longer
fit.
