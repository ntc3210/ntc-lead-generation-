# Buying signals: signs that a company is ready to buy

A good lead is two things: **a company that fits the profile** and **a
sign that now is the moment**. Fit without a signal is a cold name.
Signal without fit is a waste of time. Look for both, and save the quote
or URL that proves each one.

Ask the user which of these signals apply to their business (interview
question 12). Then turn each into searches. The channel playbooks in
`channels/` show how to search each place.

---

## Signals that work in almost every industry

| Signal | Where it shows | Why it matters | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hiring for a role your product supports or replaces** | Job boards, the company's careers page | They have the problem now, and budget for it | Strong |
| **New funding** | News, press releases, funding databases | New money, new plans, new tools | Strong for 1–6 months |
| **A new leader in the buyer's seat** (new CTO, new Head of Sales) | Press releases, company news pages | New leaders change vendors, usually in their first 90 days | Strong |
| **Public complaint about the problem you solve** | Forums, Reddit (via search), review sites | They're feeling the pain, in their own words | Strong when recent |
| **Complaint about a competitor** | Review sites, forums, community threads | They may switch | Strong |
| **Launch or expansion:** new product, new region, new office | News, the company blog | New needs | Medium |
| **A new law or regulation that hits them** | Industry news | A deadline forces a purchase | Strong near deadlines |
| **Growth:** headcount jump, new locations | Careers pages, news | Today's tools stop fitting | Medium |
| **Technology change:** moving platforms, adopting a tool that pairs with yours | Job posts naming tools, integration announcements | A natural moment to add you | Medium–strong |
| **Event activity:** speaking at or sponsoring a conference in your space | Event agendas, sponsor lists | They're investing in the area | Medium |
| **Looks like your best customers:** same industry, size, tools | Their website, case studies | Proven fit, but no timing | Fit only |

## Weak or misleading signals

- **Old posts:** a complaint from two years ago is history, not a lead.
  Check the date.
- **Big companies with in-house teams** that build rather than buy.
- **Competitors** who look like customers.
- **Agencies or consultants** who resell what you sell, unless the user
  wants partners (see `channels/partners-and-referrals.md`).
- **"Hiring" pages that never change:** some companies keep evergreen job
  ads posted.

## Grading the evidence

- **Measured:** you saw it on a page you can link (a job post, a funding
  announcement, a dated forum post).
- **Inferred:** you reasoned it from something seen ("they hired 20
  support agents, so call volume is probably growing"). Label it
  "inferred".
- **Guessed:** no source. Don't save it as a lead.

## Turning signals into searches (templates)

Replace the parts in `<angle brackets>`:
- `"<job title>" "<tool or skill>" site:<job board or careers pages>`
- `"<industry>" "raises" OR "Series A" OR "seed round" <this month>`
- `"<competitor>" "alternative" OR "switching from" OR "frustrated"`
- `"<pain phrase in the customer's words>" <forum or community>`
- `"<industry>" "appoints" OR "joins as" "<buyer title>"`

See `../examples/deepgram/profile.md`, section 5, for these filled in for
one real company.
