# Channel: content and inbound (letting buyers come to you)

> **For the assistant:** inbound means making useful things in public so
> that the right buyers find the user and raise their hand. You help pick
> topics from real questions buyers ask (with evidence), draft posts and
> answers for the user to edit and publish, and design simple free tools
> or sign-up forms with honest consent. You never publish anything
> yourself.
>
> *Practical guidance, not legal advice. Sources checked 11 October 2026.*

---

## What it is and when it works best

Instead of finding buyers, you give them a reason to find you:
- **Posts:** short articles, LinkedIn posts, newsletters, videos that
  answer a real question your buyers have.
- **Answering questions:** helpful public answers in communities and Q&A
  sites (see `communities.md` and `reddit.md` for their rules).
- **Free tools:** a calculator, checklist, template, benchmark or small
  free version of the product that solves a piece of the problem.
- **Sign-ups:** a newsletter, webinar or download that people choose to
  receive, giving you permission to follow up.

People who come to you this way are **opted in**: they asked. That makes
follow-up warmer, and legally simpler (with the consent rules below).

**Works best when:**
- Buyers research before buying (most B2B buyers, developers above all).
- The user knows things worth sharing and can write or talk about them
  every week or two.
- The user can wait: inbound builds over months, then keeps working.

**Skip it or keep it small when:**
- The user needs meetings this month (pair with an outbound channel).
- Nobody will commit to publishing regularly.

*For example:* a voice-AI API company like Deepgram might publish an
honest comparison of transcription accuracy on noisy phone audio, a free
calculator for estimating transcription costs, and code samples that
developers find through search.

## What to ask the user first

1. "What are the five questions customers ask you most before buying?"
   (Each one is a post.)
2. "What do you know that your buyers would find useful even if they
   never bought from you?"
3. "Who will publish: you, a colleague, the company account? How often,
   realistically?"
4. "Where do you already have an audience: website, LinkedIn, a
   newsletter, a YouTube channel?"
5. "Is there a small free tool you could offer (a calculator, a
   checklist, a template)?"
6. "When someone signs up or downloads something, who follows up, and
   how fast?"

## How to find leads here

Inbound finds **topics** first; leads arrive later. The assistant uses
web search to find what buyers actually ask:

- `"how do I" "<task your product helps with>"`
- `"<pain phrase>" "anyone else"` (people describing the problem)
- `site:reddit.com "<problem>"` (titles and snippets only; see
  `reddit.md`)
- `"<competitor>" "vs" OR "alternative"` (comparison questions)
- `"<industry>" "template" OR "checklist" OR "calculator" "<task>"`
  (what free tools already exist, and their gaps)

**Strong topic:** a question asked again and again, in the buyer's own
words, recently, with poor existing answers. Save three example URLs.
**Weak topic:** something the user wants to say but no buyer is asking;
a topic already covered well by big publishers.

**When leads arrive** (someone signs up, downloads, comments or emails),
record in `my-company/leads.md`: what they signed up for, the date, how
they consented (the exact checkbox wording), and what they asked.

## How to reach them

The content itself is the reach. A post template:

> **<The buyer's question, in their words>**
>
> <The short answer, first.>
> <Why it happens, in two or three sentences.>
> <How to fix it, as steps they can follow without buying anything.>
> <One honest line: "We build <product> for this. If you'd like help,
> <how to contact>.">

**Following up with someone who signed up:**
> Hi <first name>, thanks for downloading <thing>. Did it answer what you
> needed? If you're working on <problem> and want to talk it through,
> reply and I'll set up a time. If not, no need to reply.

## Step by step for the user

**Publish a post on LinkedIn (by hand):**
1. Ask the assistant for a draft on one of your five questions.
2. Edit it so it sounds like you; check every fact and number.
3. On LinkedIn, click **Start a post** at the top of your feed.
4. Paste your edited text and click **Post**.
5. Reply to comments yourself over the next day.

**Make a simple sign-up form with honest consent (Google Forms, free):**
1. Go to https://forms.google.com and click **Blank form** (the
   "+" tile).
2. Give it a title, like "Get the <thing>".
3. Add questions: **Name** and **Work email** (click **+** to add each;
   choose **Short answer**).
4. Add a **Checkboxes** question: "Yes, <company> may email me about
   <topic>. I can unsubscribe at any time." Don't make it required.
5. In the form's description, link to your privacy notice (what you
   collect, why, how to opt out).
6. Click **Send** → the link icon → **Copy**. Put that link on your
   website or post.
7. To see sign-ups, click **Responses** → **Link to Sheets**.
8. Only people who ticked the box get marketing follow-up. Everyone gets
   the thing they asked for.

**Weekly rhythm:** one post or answer a week, one follow-up batch a week,
and a monthly look at which topics brought people in.

## The rules (legal and platform)

- **Honest consent.** People who opt in can be followed up, but only for
  what they agreed to:
  - **Canada (CASL):** express consent is a clear "yes"; every message
    must identify you and offer an unsubscribe honoured within 10
    business days.
    https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email
  - **UK and EU:** consent must be specific, given by the person (the
    ICO mentions an opt-in box). The ICO's email marketing page explains
    consent and the soft opt-in (the product rule of never pre-ticking
    the box comes from this; the exact legal wording on pre-ticked boxes
    was not re-checked in this session):
    https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/electronic-mail-marketing/
  - **US (CAN-SPAM):** newsletters and follow-ups still need honest
    subjects, a postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10
    business days.
    https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
- **Disclose who you are.** When an employee recommends their company's
  product in a post or answer, they should disclose the relationship;
  a profile line "isn't enough":
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
- **Claims must be true.** Comparisons and benchmarks must be fair and
  provable. Don't publish numbers the user can't back up.
- **Website tracking.** Analytics cookies and tracking pixels need
  consent in the UK and EU. **Unverified:** I did not re-check the
  cookie rules in this session; ask the assistant to look them up before
  adding any tracking.
- **Copyright.** Don't copy other people's articles; quote briefly and
  link.

## What the assistant must never do on this channel

- Never publish, post, schedule or send anything; the user does.
- Never write fake reviews, fake testimonials, fake "customer" questions
  or posts from made-up people.
- Never invent statistics, benchmarks or quotes.
- Never design a form with a pre-ticked consent box, or hide what people
  are signing up for.
- Never add someone who only downloaded something to marketing emails
  unless they ticked the consent box.

## How to measure it

| Measure | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|
| Posts or answers published | 1 a week, every week |
| Sign-ups or downloads | rising month over month |
| Share of sign-ups who opted into email | 30% or more |
| Inbound conversations (people who contact you first) | a few a month after 2–3 months |
| Which topics bring sign-ups | note the top three; write more like them |

Review monthly in `my-company/lessons.md`. Judge inbound over a quarter,
not a week.
