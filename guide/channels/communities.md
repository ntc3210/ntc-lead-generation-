# Channel: forums, Discord, Slack and other online communities

> **For the assistant:** the rule for every community is **be a member
> first, give before you ask, and read the rules before any mention of
> the product.** The user participates by hand. You help find the right
> communities, summarise their rules (from what the user shows you), and
> draft helpful answers for the user to post. You never join, post,
> message or collect data in a community yourself. Reddit has its own
> playbook: `reddit.md`.
>
> *Practical guidance, not legal advice. Sources checked 11 October 2026.*

---

## What it is and when it works best

Online communities are groups where people with the same job, tool or
interest help each other. They include:
- **Forums:** public discussion websites (many run on software called
  Discourse), such as a product's community forum or an industry board.
- **Discord servers:** chat communities, popular with developers,
  gamers and creators.
- **Slack communities:** invite-only chat workspaces for a profession
  (e.g. marketers, data engineers, founders).
- **Facebook and LinkedIn groups, Q&A sites** (like Stack Overflow) and
  professional association member boards.

**Works best when:**
- The buyers are practitioners who learn from peers: developers, ops
  people, marketers, small-business owners, specialists in a trade.
- The user can show expertise for weeks before any sale.
- The product solves a problem people ask about in public.

**Skip it when:**
- The user wants results this month. Trust takes time here.
- No one on the team will actually take part.
- The community bans vendors (many do). Respect that and leave.

*For example:* a voice-AI API company like Deepgram might have an
engineer answer audio-processing questions in a developer Discord for
open-source speech tools, mentioning their employer only when the server
rules allow and it's relevant.

## What to ask the user first

1. "Where do your customers go to ask peers for help? Name any forum,
   Slack, Discord or group you've heard them mention."
2. "Which tools or platforms do your customers use? Their user
   communities are often the best place."
3. "Are you already a member of any of these? For how long, and are you
   active?"
4. "Who on your team knows the subject deeply and can give real
   answers?"
5. "How much time a week can that person give? (Two hours is a good
   start.)"
6. "Has any community told you not to promote? Let's write that down."

## How to find leads here

**Finding the communities** (the assistant uses web search):
- `"<industry or job title>" slack community`
- `"<tool your customers use>" discord OR forum OR community`
- `"<profession>" forum "<problem>"`
- `"join our community" "<topic>"`
- `site:<forum domain> "<pain phrase>"` (for public forums only)

**Finding conversations** on public forums: the assistant may read
individual public forum pages that come up in search, like any web page,
**unless the forum's terms forbid automated access**. Inside Discord,
Slack and private groups, only the user reads; the assistant never
accesses them.

**Strong signal:** a recent question (under 30 days) describing the
exact problem; "can anyone recommend…"; a complaint about a competitor;
someone saying their team is about to choose a tool.
**Weak signal:** old threads; general chatter; questions already well
answered; anything in a channel where vendors are told to stay out.

**What to record** in `my-company/leads.md`: the link (or, for private
communities, the community name, channel and date, as the user describes
it), the exact words of the question, and why it fits. Don't copy member
lists, usernames in bulk, or private messages into files. Only record a
business contact if the person shared it publicly for that purpose.

## How to reach them

In communities, outreach is **answering questions well, in public.**

> <Answer to the actual question, with specifics.>
> <A tip or resource that helps whether or not they buy anything.>
> <If the rules allow and it's relevant:> Disclosure: I work at
> <company>; we make <product category>. Happy to share more if useful.

If someone asks you directly for more, a private message is fine. Never
cold-message members who didn't ask, unless the community rules say
direct messages for business are welcome.

## Step by step for the user

**Joining a Discord server:**
1. Open the invite link (from the community's website).
2. Click **Accept Invite**, then log in or create a Discord account.
3. Read the **#rules** or **#welcome** channel first. Many servers make
   you click a button or react to agree.
4. Look for channels such as **#introductions**, **#help**, or
   **#promotion** or **#showcase** (the only place self-promotion may be
   allowed, if any).
5. Introduce yourself honestly: name, company, what you know about.

**Joining a Slack community:**
1. Request an invite from the community's website (often a form).
2. When the email arrives, click **Join**, and create a profile with your
   real name, role and company.
3. Open the **#rules** or **#start-here** channel, or the pinned messages
   in **#general** (click the pin icon at the top of the channel).
4. Introduce yourself in **#introductions** if there is one.

**Joining a forum:** click **Sign Up**, then find the **Guidelines**,
**FAQ** or **Rules** link (often at the top or in the footer). Read it.

**Then, for every community:**
1. Tell the assistant the rules in your own words (or paste them); it
   records them in `my-company/profile.md` under "Community rules".
2. For two to four weeks, only help: answer questions, share useful
   resources, thank people.
3. When the assistant flags a fitting question, read it yourself, ask
   for a draft, edit it, and post it yourself.

## The rules (legal and platform)

- **Each community's own rules come first.** If they ban vendors, links
  or direct messages, that's final.
- **Discord.** The Terms of Service forbid "scraping our services
  without our written consent" and "spamming, auto-messaging, or
  auto-dialing people through our services":
  https://discord.com/terms . The Community Guidelines ban "unsolicited
  bulk messages (or spam)" and say "Do not use self-bots or user-bots":
  https://discord.com/guidelines
- **Slack.** Slack's Acceptable Use Policy applies:
  https://slack.com/acceptable-use-policy . **Unverified:** I could not
  read the full text (it's in a linked document), so I can't quote its
  exact spam or scraping clauses. Each workspace also has its own rules.
- **Facebook groups.** No automated collection: Facebook Terms section
  3.2, https://www.facebook.com/legal/terms
- **Disclosure.** The FTC expects employees who mention their employer's
  products to "disclose your relationship to the company":
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
- **Personal data.** Usernames and member details are about people; under
  UK and EU GDPR, don't collect them in bulk or reuse them for marketing.

## What the assistant must never do on this channel

- Never join, post, react, vote or send messages in any community.
- Never access Discord, Slack, Facebook or other private communities, and
  never scrape any forum whose terms forbid it.
- Never suggest bots, self-bots, auto-DM tools or member-list exports.
- Never draft a reply that mentions the product before the user has
  confirmed the community's rules allow it.
- Never draft an undisclosed recommendation or a fake "happy customer"
  post.
- Never draft cold direct messages to members who didn't ask.

## How to measure it

| Measure | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|
| Helpful answers per week | 3–5 per community |
| Warnings or removed posts | 0 |
| People who reply "thanks" or ask follow-ups | rising month over month |
| Inbound questions about your company | a few a month after 6–8 weeks |
| Leads that came from a community | logged with their source in `my-company/leads.md` |

Review monthly in `my-company/lessons.md`. If after two months nobody has
engaged, try a different community before giving up on the channel.
