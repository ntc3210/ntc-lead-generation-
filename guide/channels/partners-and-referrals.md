# Channel: partners, marketplaces, integrations and referrals

> **For the assistant:** this channel borrows trust. Instead of reaching
> every buyer yourself, you reach the people and platforms buyers
> already trust: happy customers, complementary companies, consultants,
> and app marketplaces. Help the user find candidate partners (with
> evidence), plan what each side gets, and draft approaches for the user
> to approve. Never promise a partner anything on the user's behalf.
>
> *Practical guidance, not legal advice. Sources checked 11 October 2026.*

---

## What it is and when it works best

Four kinds of partner:
1. **Referrals from customers:** happy customers introduce you to peers.
   The warmest lead there is.
2. **Complementary companies:** businesses that sell something different
   to the same buyer (an accounting firm and a payroll tool, for
   instance). You can refer customers to each other.
3. **Resellers, agencies and consultants:** people who advise your
   buyers and can recommend or resell you.
4. **Marketplaces and integrations:** app stores and directories inside
   platforms your buyers already use (for example a cloud provider's
   marketplace, a CRM's app directory, or an e-commerce platform's app
   store). Building an integration can put you in front of every user of
   that platform.

**Works best when:**
- The user has at least a few happy customers.
- Their buyers already rely on a platform, adviser or other supplier.
- The product is easy for a partner to explain in one sentence.

**Skip it or wait when:**
- The user has no customers yet (referrals need happy customers; get a
  few through other channels first).
- The product is still changing every week (partners hate surprises).

*For example:* a voice-AI API company like Deepgram might list itself in
a cloud marketplace, partner with contact-centre platforms that need
transcription, and ask agencies that build voice bots to recommend it.

## What to ask the user first

1. "Who are your three happiest customers? Would they introduce you to
   someone if asked?"
2. "What else do your customers buy, from whom, at the same time as
   you? Those companies are candidate partners."
3. "Who advises your customers before they buy: consultants, agencies,
   accountants, integrators?"
4. "Which software platforms do your customers use every day? Do any
   have an app marketplace or partner directory?"
5. "What can you offer a partner: a referral fee, a discount for their
   clients, referrals back, or joint marketing?" (Don't promise anything
   the user hasn't decided.)
6. "Is your industry regulated in a way that limits referral fees?"
   (Some are: see "The rules".)

## How to find leads here

**Complementary companies and advisers** (the assistant uses web search):
- `"<platform your customers use>" "partner directory" OR "find a partner"`
- `"<industry>" "consultant" OR "agency" "<your problem area>"`
- `"<tool that pairs with yours>" "integrations"` (who already
  integrates with that tool)
- `"<industry>" "preferred vendor" OR "recommended partners"`
- `"<type of agency>" "we use" "<tool category>"`

**Marketplaces:**
- `"<platform>" marketplace "<your category>"` (see who's listed and how
  they describe themselves)
- `"<platform>" "partner program" "apply"`

**Customer referrals:** start from the user's own customer list, not
search. The assistant helps decide whom to ask and when.

**Strong signal:** a company that already partners with others in your
space; an agency whose case studies show your buyer type; a platform
whose users keep asking for what you do (look for feature requests or
forum threads).
**Weak signal:** a huge company with no partner programme; an agency
that resells your competitor exclusively; a "partner" page that hasn't
changed in years.

**What to record** in `my-company/leads.md`: partner name, type
(customer, complementary, adviser, marketplace), the URL showing why
they fit, what they'd gain, and the status.

## How to reach them

**Asking a happy customer for a referral** (the user sends it):
> Hi <first name>, I'm glad <result they got> has worked out. Do you know
> anyone at another <type of company> who's dealing with <problem>? If
> so, would you be comfortable introducing us? No pressure at all.

**Approaching a complementary company:**
> Hi <first name>, we both sell to <shared buyer type>: you with <their
> product>, us with <yours>. Our customers often ask about <their
> category>. Would it be worth a call to see if referring customers to
> each other makes sense?

**Approaching an adviser or agency:**
> Hi <first name>, I saw on <URL> that you help <type of client> with
> <area>. Some of your clients may run into <problem>; we solve that.
> Would a 20-minute walkthrough be useful, so you know whether we're
> worth recommending?

## Step by step for the user

**Ask for referrals (start this week):**
1. Ask the assistant to draft a referral request for each of your three
   happiest customers.
2. Read, edit and approve each one.
3. Send it yourself, from your normal email or by phone.
4. When someone introduces you, reply within a day and thank the
   introducer.

**Apply to a marketplace or partner programme:**
1. Ask the assistant for the platform's partner or marketplace page, with
   the requirements and **any costs** listed.
2. Open the page and look for **Become a partner**, **Apply** or
   **List your app**.
3. Read the partner agreement before signing. The assistant can summarise
   it, but it is not legal advice; for anything with fees, exclusivity or
   liability, ask a lawyer.
4. Fill in the application yourself.

**Keep a partner list** in `my-company/leads.md` with a status for each:
idea, contacted, talking, agreed, active, not now.

## The rules (legal and platform)

- **Disclose paid relationships.** If a partner earns a fee for
  recommending you, their recommendation must make that clear. The FTC's
  Endorsement Guides FAQ explains disclosure of relationships, for
  example that employees "should disclose your relationship to the
  company":
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking
  (I read the employee section; the broader point that any paid
  relationship needs disclosure is from the same Guides but I did not
  re-read that passage: treat as **unverified** wording.)
- **Referral fees in regulated industries.** Some industries limit or
  ban paying for referrals (for example US real estate settlement
  services and healthcare). **Unverified:** I did not check these laws
  in this session. If the user is in finance, insurance, healthcare,
  property or law, they must ask a lawyer before offering fees.
- **Marketplace terms.** Every marketplace has its own listing rules,
  fees and review process. **Unverified per platform:** read the
  specific programme's terms.
- **Contacting partners** follows the rules of the channel used
  (`cold-email.md`, `cold-calling.md`, `linkedin.md`).
- **Customer data.** Don't share one customer's details with a partner
  without that customer's permission (UK/EU GDPR and plain trust).

## What the assistant must never do on this channel

- Never promise a fee, discount, exclusivity or joint plan the user
  hasn't decided.
- Never sign up, apply or agree to anything on the user's behalf.
- Never share customer names or data with a partner without permission.
- Never draft a referral request that pressures a customer, or one
  offering an undisclosed payment.
- Never claim a partnership exists before it does ("as a <platform>
  partner…").

## How to measure it

| Measure | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|
| Referral requests sent to happy customers | at least 3 a month |
| Introductions received | 1 in 3 requests |
| Active partners who send at least one lead a quarter | growing slowly |
| Leads by source (customer, partner, marketplace) | tracked in `my-company/leads.md` |
| Win rate on partner leads vs cold leads | usually higher; check it |

Review quarterly in `my-company/lessons.md`. Partnerships are slow to
start and slow to stop, so judge them over months.
