# Channel: events, conferences and webinars

> **For the assistant:** events are where buyers show up in person (or
> online) on purpose, ready to talk. Use public agendas, speaker lists
> and sponsor lists to find companies and people who fit, help the user
> plan who to meet, and draft follow-ups for the user to approve. Never
> buy or scrape attendee lists.
>
> *Practical guidance, not legal advice. Sources checked 11 October 2026.*

---

## What it is and when it works best

Three ways to use events:
1. **Attend:** go to a conference, trade show or meetup where buyers
   gather, and meet them.
2. **Research:** read the public agenda, speaker list and sponsor list
   to find companies that are investing in the area. (A company that
   sponsors or speaks on a topic cares about it.)
3. **Host:** run a webinar (an online talk) or small meetup that the
   right people sign up for. Sign-ups are people who opted in, which
   makes follow-up much easier legally (see `inbound.md`).

**Works best when:**
- The industry has strong events: trade shows, regional associations,
  developer conferences, user groups for a platform.
- The deal size justifies travel or a sponsorship, or the user can
  attend local and online events cheaply.
- The user is comfortable talking to strangers.

**Skip it or keep it small when:**
- Budget is tight and the only events are expensive. Start with free
  meetups and webinars.
- Buyers don't attend events in this industry.

*For example:* a voice-AI API company like Deepgram might review the
speaker list of a contact-centre industry conference, find product
leaders talking about "AI agents for customer service", and ask to meet
them at the event.

## What to ask the user first

1. "Which events do your customers go to? Trade shows, association
   meetings, user groups, online summits?"
2. "What's your budget for events this quarter? (Tickets, travel,
   sponsorship.)" Say the cost of anything before recommending it.
3. "Would you rather attend, speak, sponsor, or host your own webinar?"
4. "Which cities or time zones are practical?"
5. "Have you been to any before? What happened?"
6. "Is there a talk or demo you could give that teaches something
   useful, not a sales pitch?"

## How to find leads here

**Finding events** (the assistant uses web search):
- `"<industry>" conference <year> "<region>"`
- `"<industry>" "trade show" OR "expo" <year>`
- `"<job title>" summit <year>`
- `"<tool your customers use>" "user conference" OR "user group"`
- `"<industry>" meetup "<city>"`
- `"<topic>" webinar <month year>`

**Finding companies and people at an event** (public pages only):
- The event's **agenda** or **speakers** page: who is talking about the
  problem you solve.
- The **sponsors** or **exhibitors** page: who is spending money in
  this area.
- `site:<event domain> "<topic>"` to search one event's site.

**Strong signal:** a speaker whose talk title describes your problem; a
company sponsoring an event in your niche; a company exhibiting a
product that integrates with yours.
**Weak signal:** a large generic attendee count; companies that only
appear in a logo wall from previous years; events with no published
agenda.

**What to record** in `my-company/leads.md`: event name and date, the
agenda or sponsor page URL, the talk title or listing as written, the
person's business role, and the plan (meet at booth, attend talk, ask
for a meeting).

## How to reach them

**Before the event** (through `cold-email.md` or `linkedin.md`):
> Hi <first name>, I saw you're speaking at <event> on <talk title>.
> I'll be there; we work on <related problem> for <type of company>.
> Would you have 15 minutes for a coffee on <day>? If not, I'll look
> forward to the talk.

**After meeting someone in person** (they gave you their card or
details, so mention it):
> Hi <first name>, good to meet you at <event> on <day>. You mentioned
> <what they said>. Here's the <thing you promised>. Would a call next
> week help?

**After a webinar you hosted** (to people who registered):
> Thanks for joining <webinar title>. Here's the recording and the
> slides: <link>. If you'd like to talk about <topic> for your team,
> reply and I'll set up a time.

## Step by step for the user

**Planning an event visit:**
1. Ask the assistant for a shortlist of events with dates, prices and a
   one-line reason each. It must tell you the cost first.
2. Pick one. Buy your ticket on the event's own website.
3. Ask the assistant for a "who to meet" list from the public speakers
   and sponsors pages, each with the reason.
4. Approve any meeting requests it drafts, and send them yourself.
5. At the event, write a one-line note after each conversation (on your
   phone is fine): name, company, what they said, what you promised.
6. Paste your notes into the chat afterwards. The assistant drafts
   follow-ups for you to approve.

**Hosting a free webinar (using Zoom as an example; other tools work
similarly, and menu names can change, so check the tool's help pages if
a button isn't where described):**
1. Sign in to Zoom on the web. Click **Webinars** (needs a paid webinar
   add-on) or **Meetings** → **Schedule a Meeting** and turn on
   **Registration**.
2. On the registration form, add a checkbox: "Yes, <company> may email
   me about related topics." Leave it unticked by default.
3. Share the registration link in your own channels (`inbound.md`).
4. After the event, export registrants (**Reports** or the meeting's
   **Registration** tab) and give the file to the assistant. It marks
   who ticked the box; only those get marketing follow-up.

## The rules (legal and platform)

- **Don't buy attendee lists.** The UK regulator says bought lists may
  only be used for emails or texts if each person specifically consented
  to messages from **you**; "Generic consent covering any third party
  will not be enough."
  https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/using-marketing-lists/
  Many "attendee lists" sold by email are also scams.
- **Follow-up email is still marketing.** CAN-SPAM (no B2B exemption,
  postal address, opt-out honoured within 10 business days):
  https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business
  ; PECR and UK GDPR, EU rules, Germany's UWG, and Canada's CASL all
  apply as in `cold-email.md`.
- **Business cards and badge scans.** Someone who hands you a card for
  follow-up expects a follow-up about what you discussed. For badge
  scans, the organiser's terms decide what you may send. **Unverified:**
  this varies by event; check the exhibitor agreement.
- **Event rules.** Many events ban soliciting in sessions or in the
  event app if you aren't a sponsor. Read the code of conduct.

## What the assistant must never do on this channel

- Never buy, scrape or use an attendee list, or recommend one.
- Never scrape event apps or attendee directories.
- Never recommend spending money without stating the cost and getting
  the user's yes.
- Never send a follow-up the user hasn't approved word for word.
- Never claim the user met someone they didn't, or invent what was said.
- Never market to webinar registrants who didn't tick the opt-in, beyond
  sending what they registered for (the recording).

## How to measure it

| Measure | What "working" looks like |
|---|---|
| Planned meetings booked before the event | 3–10 per event |
| Real conversations at the event | 10–30 |
| Follow-ups that get a reply | 30% or more |
| Meetings or opportunities within 30 days | at least a few per event |
| Cost per real opportunity | compare across events; drop the worst |
| Webinar: registrants who attend | 30–50% |

Write each event's cost and results into `my-company/lessons.md`, so the
next event decision uses real numbers.
