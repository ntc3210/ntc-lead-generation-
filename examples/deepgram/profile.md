# Worked example: Deepgram (voice AI)

> **This is a teaching example**, built only from Deepgram's public
> website and public news, researched 11 October 2026. It is not
> affiliated with, endorsed by, or written for Deepgram. It shows Lead
> Scout what a *finished* company profile looks like for a business that
> sells to other businesses. Facts may have changed since, so check the
> source before relying on any of them.
>
> **V** = found on the linked page (in a real profile, write
> **[found: URL]**). **I** = inferred from what was seen (in a real
> profile, **[guess]**). Real profiles also use **[user]** for what the
> user said. See `templates/company-profile.md`.

## 1. Who they are
- **What they sell (V, https://deepgram.com):** voice AI for developers
  and enterprises, as four APIs (application programming interfaces:
  ways for other companies' software to use theirs):
  - speech-to-text (Nova-3, Flux);
  - text-to-speech (Aura-2);
  - a Voice Agent API that combines both with a language model;
  - audio intelligence (sentiment, topics, summaries).
  Self-hosting is also available.
- **How it's bought (V, https://deepgram.com/pricing):**
  - **Pay As You Go:** self-serve, $200 free credit, no card needed.
  - **Growth:** prepaid from $4K a year.
  - **Enterprise:** custom pricing, with dedicated support, self-hosting
    and HIPAA agreements.
  - **Usage pricing**, for example speech-to-text streaming at about
    $0.0048 per minute.
- **Size and stage (V, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260113406092/en/):**
  a $130M Series C at a $1.3B valuation, January 2026.

## 2. Ideal customer
- **Industries (V, from https://deepgram.com/customers and solution pages):**
  - contact centres and contact-centre software;
  - voice-agent and conversational-AI startups;
  - healthcare (medical scribes, patient-call agents);
  - financial services and collections;
  - telecom and communications platforms;
  - sales and revenue software;
  - media;
  - recruiting;
  - US public sector.
- **Two size clusters (I):**
  - **startups and scale-ups** that build voice into their own product
    (most logos);
  - **large platforms and enterprises** (e.g. Five9, IBM, an unnamed
    Fortune 50 pharmacy, all V).
- **Regions (V, https://deepgram.com/careers):** sales roles in the US,
  EMEA (London, Switzerland) and APAC (Singapore, Sydney, Melbourne).
- **Buyer roles (I, from the job titles quoted in case studies, which are V):**
  - **User and champion:** developers and ML engineers.
  - **Decider:** VP Product, Head of Data, CTO.
  - **Signer:** at startups, often the founder or CEO.
  - **Influencers in contact centres:** QA, compliance and CX operations
    leaders.
- **Not a fit (I):** companies with no voice or audio in their product
  or operations; very small teams with no developers.

## 3. Problem and moment
- **Problems, in customers' words (V, case studies):**
  - **CallTrackingMetrics:** "too inaccurate" transcripts (about 40%
    unusable before switching).
  - **Revenue.io:** jargon its old model got wrong.
  - **Five9:** capturing account numbers and VINs.
- **Moments that start a search (I):**
  - launching a voice bot, AI receptionist or AI scribe;
  - replacing an IVR (the "press 1 for…" phone menu);
  - new QA or compliance requirements in a contact centre;
  - complaints about accuracy, latency (delay) or cost with a current
    provider;
  - fresh funding for a voice-AI startup;
  - expanding into new languages.
- **Competitors (V, G2 comparison pages):** AssemblyAI, Speechmatics,
  ElevenLabs. Also, inferred: big cloud providers' own speech services,
  OpenAI Whisper.

## 4. Deal
- **Range (I, from pricing):** from small self-serve accounts up to
  enterprise contracts. Self-serve converts through the product itself;
  bigger deals go through sales.
- **Sales motion (V, careers page):**
  - Account executives by segment: Corporate, Strategic, Federal, EMEA,
    APAC.
  - SDRs (sales development reps, who do first-touch outreach): two
    roles, so some outbound exists.
  - Many solutions engineers.

## 8. What this example teaches
- A company like this has **two kinds of buyer at once**: developers who
  try it themselves, and enterprises that need sales. The channel plan
  has to serve both. See `channel-plan.md` in this folder.
- **The best signals are technical** (job posts naming tools, community
  questions). That's why communities and job posts beat cold calls here.
- **Partners matter.** Many customers arrive through platforms they
  already use.
